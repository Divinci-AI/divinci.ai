Benchmark your own AI

The rows above are Divinci's configurations. The same signed pipeline runs on yours: your models, your retrieval stacks, and ensembles of retrievers working together, scored on your own question sets or on the platform benchmarks.

Track it privately

Every run is private unless you choose otherwise. Score each release as you ship it, see its rating move over time, and catch a regression before your users do. Private runs are signed too, so you can verify them yourself.

Publish what's new

Make a run public and anyone can verify its signed manifest. Its outputs are checked for personal identifiers first, and answers that are already public are refused. Runs on the platform benchmarks join the boards here; a board built from your own questions stays private unless you ask us to list it.

What these boards do not claim

  • Attestation says this score is real. It does not say this model is safe. Evidence, not a guarantee.
  • The retrieval boards' runs are republished rather than measured: the scores were computed by Divinci's scored-QA pipeline and republished into a signed TrustRun. The verifier warns you about exactly this, and the manifests say so themselves.
  • Several platform benchmarks are too small to rank on and are not published here. A benchmark with one sample can only ever score 0.0 or 1.0.
  • The retrieval boards use one answering model, glm-5.3-flash. They do not all use one judge: every row is judged by gemini-2.5-flash except PageIndex + Jev on the nutrition board, which was judged by gemini-3.8-flash. Scores from different judges are not comparable, so a board with more than one judge shows one tab per judge, each ranked on its own. On the same answers, 3.8 Flash scores these rows about 1 to 5 points higher than 2.5 Flash. That changes the row's number but not its place: under 2.5 Flash the same configuration's median over four passes is 0.823, still between Vectorize and plain PageIndex. Each signed manifest names its judge in its metric.
  • On the nutrition board the Vertex row searches a newer, larger ingestion than the others.
  • PixelRAG + Jev, first on the nutrition board, was designed on the same sixty questions it is scored on. It also indexes every rendered page of the corpus's eighteen PDFs, where the other rows hold only the passages their parser kept, and it ran from a laptop. Its lead over Vertex holds under three judges and is small under all of them. The write-up has the details.
  • PixelRAG, no Jev is the same retriever with Jev's ranking step removed: 0.817 against 0.947, same judge. PageIndex + Jev, half a point below it, was scored by a different judge and sits in its own tab, so the two are not ranked against each other. Rows ranked by Jev carry TypeSafe AI's mark.
