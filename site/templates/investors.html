2Problem

The EU AI Act's enforcement powers went live on 2 August 2026. GDPR's right to erasure GDPR Art. 17 was already enforceable, with fines up to €20M or 4% of global turnover.

A company can delete a database row, but a model (software that learned patterns from data) can still remember a person in its learned numerical memory. "We deleted the row" is no longer a complete answer. Regulators (EDPB, CNIL) currently treat retraining as the defensible response — slow, expensive, and in practice rarely done. Most large enterprises running AI in Europe have no answer at all if a model-level erasure demand arrives.

To our knowledge no regulator has yet ruled on whether any unlearning method discharges GDPR Article 17. That question is open for everyone in this market.

3Solution and benefits

Find. Remove. Prove. Identify what a model learned about an individual; remove it on open-weight models; re-measure performance against thresholds set in advance; issue a signed receipt (Ed25519, a standard digital-signature method) served from a public endpoint and checked by an open-source verifier that has no dependency on Divinci.

4Target market

Privacy, DPO and GRC buyers at organisations running open-weight, self-hosted, EU-facing models with a live or imminent erasure exposure. Our honest estimate of that qualified market is tens to low hundreds of organisations globally, unmeasured — sizing it through 15–20 privacy-counsel and DPO interviews is a task inside the 30-day gate, not an assumption.

Context, not plan: the machine-unlearning niche is projected at $0.15B (2025) → $2.73B (2032), 51.2% CAGR, a market-research extrapolation on a small base. Fortune 500 AI-compliance programmes budget $8–15M initially.

10Competitive advantage

The defensible position is the bundle: baseline → erase → re-measure → attest, as one verifiable artifact. Hirundo (Israel, $8M seed, weight-level edits on Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen and Gemma) is ahead on distribution and could copy a receipt format in weeks, but is behind on EU framing and, as far as we can see, on the evidence artifact. Rank-one editing methods (ROME, MEMIT) are published commodity; the moat is not the edit, it is the measured, signed, independently checkable evidence around it.

We publish our failures. That is credibility, not a moat, and we say so. Divinci holds a registered trademark; no patents are filed, and a freedom-to-operate review is budgeted.
