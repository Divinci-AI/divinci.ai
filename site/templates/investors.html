2Problem

GDPR's right to erasure GDPR Art. 17 has been enforceable since 2018, with fines up to €20M or 4% of global turnover. The EU AI Act's strict high-risk rules were pushed back by the Digital Omnibus Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 to December 2027 and August 2028, so we do not rely on the AI Act for urgency.

A company can delete a database row, but a model (software that learned patterns from data) can still remember a person in its learned numerical memory. "We deleted the row" is no longer a complete answer. Regulators (EDPB, CNIL) currently treat retraining as the defensible response — slow, expensive, and in practice rarely done. Most large enterprises running AI in Europe have no answer at all if a model-level erasure demand arrives.

To our knowledge no regulator has yet ruled on whether any unlearning method discharges GDPR Article 17. That question is open for everyone in this market.

3Solution and benefits

Find. Remove. Prove. Identify what a model learned about an individual; remove it on open-weight models; re-measure performance against thresholds set in advance; issue a signed receipt (Ed25519, a standard digital-signature method) served from a public endpoint and checked by an open-source verifier that has no dependency on Divinci.

  • Evidence an auditor can check alone. The receipt is live in production today, not a proposed format.
  • The proof step is the product. In our own August test the untouched control topics degraded more than the erased topic (−0.058 vs −0.026). A vendor that cannot measure that ships the damage silently. Nobody else sells the measurement half.

4Target market

Privacy, DPO and GRC buyers at organisations running open-weight, self-hosted, EU-facing models with a live or imminent erasure exposure. Our honest estimate of that qualified market is tens to low hundreds of organisations globally, unmeasured — sizing it through 15–20 privacy-counsel and DPO interviews is a task inside the 30-day gate, not an assumption.

Context, not plan: the machine-unlearning niche is projected at $0.15B (2025) → $2.73B (2032), 51.2% CAGR, a market-research extrapolation on a small base. We do not plan against it; a bottom-up count of reachable organisations is a task of the gate.

5Traction: what is proved, and what is not yet

Proved — built and measured.

  • TrustBench receipt in production; public key registry live; first certificate externally verified. QA suite in production with per-metric threshold gates, scheduled runs and alerts.
  • Erasure API (LarQL) in staging on open-weight models: two API surfaces, two SDKs, a CLI and a web UI. Edits are reversible: removing a patch restores the original model byte for byte.
  • Measurement that catches damage, including our own. The August test caught untouched topics degrading more than the erased one. On 24 September a wider check of an edit from our shipping toolchain found that, although the file changed in one row and restores byte for byte, it damaged 8 of 100 other facts and about one neighbouring capital in five. We report that rather than call such edits surgical.
  • A person-level test protocol with pass/fail criteria set in advance, run on one small open model (Gemma 4 E2B). Records about five made-up people were fine-tuned in, then removed by rebuilding the fine-tune without them from the verified public base, and compared with six control models that never saw them. The base is verified, the result can be re-derived independently, and every informative recovery attack on the removed people fails (9 of 9).
  • Facts inserted with our own editing tool delete exactly: the deleted model hashes identical to the original.
  • Divinci platform in production. Seven software-consultancy partnerships signed.

Not yet proved.

  • The person-level test has not passed. Its "still a usable model" check is undecided: instruction-following at 0.931× the base, interval 0.862–0.983 against a 0.90 bar. By the rule set before the run, the recovery-attack result counts as erasure evidence only once that check passes. The model that learned the people sits on the same margin, so the fine-tuning recipe is the likely cause, but the bar does not move. It has run on one model family only.
  • Cheaper removal than a rebuild: gradient-unlearning methods have not met their pre-set bars, and edits do not remove a fine-tuned-in person.
  • Deleting a fact the model learned in pretraining suppresses it rather than removing it: our strongest deletion of the Paris fact returned after two small retraining steps. We make no erasure claim for pretraining data.
  • No buyer interviews, design partners, or introductions from the consultancy partners yet. No measured cost per erasure; today we know only compute time, 3.4–6.7 GPU-hours on one cloud GPU per test run.

The 30-day gate. Every line is pass/fail against criteria written down before it runs.

  • Person-level erasure passes all five checks on Gemma, then on a mid-sized model from a second family (target: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash). Any failed or undecided check is a fail.
  • An independent German AI engineering firm designs and runs an adversarial test of the result, and a German technology and data-protection law firm writes an opinion on GDPR Article 17 and EU AI Act Article 53(1)(c) based on it. About €30k, capped; roughly two weeks once the test case is ready.
  • 15–20 interviews with privacy, DPO and AI leads on budget holder, purchase trigger and price. Three design partners signed, each with a written scope, success criteria and a paid next step.
  • Consultancy partner activity counted per partner: introductions, meetings, qualified prospects.
  • The full cost of one erasure on the second model, and a bottom-up count of reachable organisations.

10Competitive advantage

The defensible position is the bundle: baseline → erase → re-measure → attest, as one verifiable artifact. Hirundo (Israel, $8M seed, weight-level edits on Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen and Gemma) is ahead on distribution and could copy a receipt format in weeks, but is behind on EU framing and, as far as we can see, on the evidence artifact. Rank-one editing methods (ROME, MEMIT) are published commodity; the moat is not the edit, it is the measured, signed, independently checkable evidence around it.

We publish our failures. That is credibility, not a moat, and we say so. Divinci holds a registered trademark; no patents are filed, and a freedom-to-operate review is budgeted.
