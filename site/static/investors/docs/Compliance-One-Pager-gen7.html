Erasure you can prove

A company can delete a database row, but a model (software that learned patterns from data) can still remember a person in its learned numerical memory. GDPR Article 17 grants erasure of personal data [REG-01]. Rebuilding the model clears that memory, but the same expensive rebuild serves every future request. Divinci plans to erase the remembered item, measure the rest, and issue a receipt an outsider can check. The bracketed codes throughout point to the sourced fact behind each number.

What exists today

  • A verifiable receipt is in production. It uses Ed25519, a standard digital-signature method, and an independently licensed tool can check it [PROD-01].
  • The erasure engine is staging only, not live, and it does not affect current chat traffic [PROD-04].
  • We report a failed public test. Our own check caught the damage: removing one document dropped its retrieval from 4/4 to 0, and overall accuracy slipped about five points (0.926 to 0.876). The topics we had not touched — the controls — slipped more than the one we erased (-0.058 versus -0.026), which is exactly what the check exists to catch. That test was not model weights (learned numerical memory) and not personal data [PROD-09].

This is early. To our knowledge, no regulator has accepted or rejected model-level unlearning for GDPR Article 17 [REG-06]. Our interpretation is a product thesis, not a legal citation.

The 30-day gate

Before commercial claims, we will pre-register and publish one falsifiable protocol — thresholds written down before the test, so no one can move them after seeing the result. For one individual, erased answers must remain 0 after direct and reworded prompts. Assumption: the planning-grade general-accuracy threshold is a 0.05 drop. The kill criterion — the result that stops the programme — is any breach of that threshold; a failed gate stops the programme, not a sales plan. The protocol covers multiple association types (each kind of link the model holds between a person and a fact, such as a name-to-diagnosis pair) and at least two open-weight model families (models whose learned numerical memory can be inspected and changed), plus a Control Individual — a matched person we never target — whose data is never erased. One individual is an n = 1 mechanical test; it cannot establish transfer.

The gate also includes a live GDPR Article 17 request: a design partner sends a real request, we attempt to discharge it, and we record the result and receipt. Counsel reviews the interpretation, but counsel opinion is a bounding input, not a ruling that resolves the experiment [GATE-01].

Who buys first

The first buyer is a privacy, DPO (data-protection officer), or GRC (governance, risk, and compliance) team with an EU-facing model and a real GDPR Article 17 exposure. We start with three design partners, not a broad channel [STR-01, STR-02].

Cost

The founder-scale 30-day gate costs about $224k [MON-01]. Programme revenue in year 1 is $0 [MON-02]. That is a gate, not the ~$1.5M full-year programme; the larger plan is justified only after the gate result [MON-03].

Why us

  • The receipt is shipped, independently checkable, and not a proposed format [PROD-01].
  • We report damage, not only wins. Our own failed corpus experiment showed that controls degraded more than the erased topic [PROD-09].
  • The defensible bundle is baseline, erase, re-measure, and receipt in one artifact.

The ask

No capital. We need:

  1. Three design partners with EU exposure and model access.
  2. An introduction to one EU data-protection counsel for the scoped question.
  3. One named academic willing to test the result adversarially and co-sign it if it holds.

Citation layer (why you will see two "Article 17"s below): GDPR Article 17 is the erasure right [REG-01]; EU AI Act Article 17 is quality management [REG-02]; Annex IV is the AI Act's technical-documentation checklist; it is not the same statute and contains no GDPR Article 17 erasure clause [REG-03].
