Pre-registered 30-day gate protocol

This section is the pre-registered gate protocol [GATE-01]. Its metric names, thresholds, decision rules, kill criterion, transfer matrix, control individual, and request-test scope are fixed in advance, before the individual-level result exists. Assumption: the thresholds below are planning-grade decision thresholds for this gate, not measured product claims.

Metrics and thresholds. For every target/control cell, measure target association recall, paraphrase-restatement recall, control-individual recall, held-out QA score, and QA-suite minScore/maxDrift results [PROD-03]. In each cell, target association recall measures the named association directly, while paraphrase-restatement recall applies the fixed rewording probe to that same association; the latter is one probe applied across all cells, not a fourth association type. Assumption: the gate passes erasure only when target association recall and paraphrase-restatement recall each fall to <0.05 of baseline, control-individual recall stays ≥0.95, held-out QA score remains ≥0.95, and maxDrift remains ≤0.05; these are planning-grade decision thresholds, not measured product results.

Decision rule. Go only if every planned transfer cell satisfies the erasure thresholds and no control or utility threshold fails. No-go if any planned transfer cell leaves target or paraphrase recall at ≥0.05, or if any control or utility threshold fails. Kill criterion: if a targeted individual remains recoverable through the pre-registered paraphrase probe at ≥0.05, or control/general utility breaches its threshold, the individual-erasure programme is killed for this gate; it does not advance to channel expansion until a new pre-registered protocol is agreed.

Transfer matrix. The matrix is the pre-registered matrix for this gate; it varies model family and association type. Assumption: it uses three open-weight model families—Llama, Qwen, and Gemma—because these families are represented in the competitive landscape [MKT-07], and three distinct association types—name↔diagnosis, name↔address, and name↔employer—so the 3×3 design is nine planned model/association cells. Each cell runs five targeted individuals plus Control Individual C0, meaning 45 targeted erasures across the matrix; five is a planning-grade minimum for existence evidence, not a power calculation. The paraphrase-restatement probe is applied to every cell as part of the single probe set; it is not counted as an association type. This matrix is a test specification and does not assert that erasure works on any model. It includes Control Individual C0, a matched individual who is never targeted. If target recall falls but Control Individual C0 also falls beyond its threshold, the result is recorded as general degradation, not successful erasure. One individual cannot establish transfer; n=1 is reported as existence evidence only.

Counsel scope. The scoped EU counsel opinion is an input that bounds the argument: it identifies conditions, risk limits, and wording Divinci may and may not claim. It does not settle, resolve, decide, or adjudicate GDPR Article 17 [GATE-01, REG-01]. To our knowledge, no regulator has adjudicated whether any unlearning method discharges GDPR Article 17 [REG-06].

Live request test. In the same 30-day window, a consenting design partner submits a real GDPR Article 17 request, and the team attempts end-to-end discharge in the erasure staging environment [PROD-04], using sealed/irreversible mode as the required privacy-facing workstream [STR-04]. The deliverable is a dated request, the attempted discharge, the post-erasure QA result, and the signed receipt where one is issued [PROD-01]. Assumption: partner consent and a partner-supplied live request are gate inputs; this is a planned test, not a measured event.

2. The regulatory foundation (verified against the primary source)

Sigma pulled the official text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and verified directly: the EU AI Act Article 17's literal title is 'Quality management system'; Annex IV is 'the technical documentation referred to in Article 11'; the word 'erasure' appears zero times in the Act. The AI Act never mentions a right to erasure. This document therefore splits the obligations exactly as the statute does.

2.1 GDPR Article 17 — the erasure driver (the wedge)

  • Data subjects can demand erasure of personal data 'without undue delay' — practically one month, extendable to three. GDPR fines reach €20M or 4% of global turnover.
  • EDPB Opinion 28/2024: models from which personal data can be extracted are not anonymous — the GDPR applies to the model itself. EDPB's Jan 2025 report 'Effective implementation of data subjects' rights' maps retraining, exact unlearning (SISA-style), and the evidence problem — the EU authority's own map of this market.
  • CNIL guidance: retraining is the defensible route today; output filters as fallback. No regulator has accepted or rejected unlearning as satisfying GDPR Article 17 — that is the open adjudication question the plan must resolve via counsel opinion and a defensible demonstration, not assume away.
  • Honest scope: 'We deleted the row' is a complete answer when personal data lives in a RAG store or retrieval corpus. The model-level demand only bites where the data is actually memorized in weights — a narrower condition than most framing implies, and the plan's TAM filters (§3) are built on it.

2.2 EU AI Act Annex IV — documentation (supporting asset only)

  • Annex IV (via Art 11): nine sections of technical documentation for high-risk systems, kept current for 10 years. Section 2(d) covers training-data datasheets (provenance, selection, cleaning) — facts about the training pipeline. Section 2(g) covers validation/testing, metrics, signed test logs.
  • Opus 5's cut: vIndex observes the trained artifact; it cannot generate training-pipeline provenance — that lives in the customer's data-lineage systems (which OneTrust, Collibra, IBM already sell). Selling 'Annex IV documentation from vIndex' oversells by an order of magnitude and will be caught by the first Big 4 practitioner.
  • Sigma's cut: the Annex IV documentation lane is already crowded and commoditizing — open-source generators (ai-act-conformity-pack, EuConform, VerifyWise, Compl-AI, MCP EU AI Act Scanner) already emit auditor-ready evidence packages; Vanta, FairNow, Kertos, Hyperproof, ComplyACT AI sell EU-AI-Act-specific compliance. vIndex-as-dossier is not unique.
  • The genuine fit is narrow and honest: a LarQL receipt is a legitimate signed test log under 2(g). That is one bullet in the erasure deck, not a second GTM motion.
  • Deadlines (post-Digital Omnibus, Reg 2026/1744): Annex III high-risk obligations 2 Dec 2027; Annex I 2 Aug 2028. Enforcement powers live since 2 Aug 2026; Art 50 transparency marking grace ends 2 Dec 2026.

5. The wedge and the one story

  • The product (company-published, divinci.ai/compliance): vIndex maps a model's learned associations (entity → relation → target, with weight/layer/feature); LarQL is the query/patch language over it. A LarQL patch is portable JSON; the patch-level content checksum is a REQUIRED WORKSTREAM (PROD-06: only the vIndex artifact sha256 exists in code today). Nine published vIndexes (sixteen catalogued, one served) across Gemma, Qwen, Llama, Mistral, OpenAI MoE, Microsoft 1-bit. Open-weights only, by design.
  • The contradiction, and the fix: 'apply to suppress, remove to restore bit-for-bit' is reversibility — a feature for model editing, an anti-feature for erasure. GDPR Article 17 buyers need the data gone, not switchable. Required before any privacy-facing sale: sealed/irreversible mode (patch applied, restoration key destroyed or never generated, receipt retained). The receipt then attests to an operation that cannot be undone — the only version the GDPR Article 17 story survives. Estimate: days of engineering, not months.
  • The receipt's honest scope: a SHA-256 checksum proves the patch was not altered; it does not by itself prove the data is gone. Efficacy must be evidenced by the probe protocol (before/after extraction, membership-inference, paraphrase, multi-hop, relearning-resistance) — and published (see §8). A signed, publicly verifiable receipt is a liability document if the claim is later falsified: scope attestation language and carry E&O insurance before receipt #1 leaves the building.
  • Positioning that is defensible today, without waiting on a regulator: 'Find where personal data lives in your open-weights model, remove its elicitability, and produce independently verifiable evidence of what you did.' Assert what can be proven; let counsel argue sufficiency under GDPR Article 17.
  • Certification status (company-published): vIndex dossier is 'a draft for your assessment, not a declaration of conformity'; SOC 2 not certified; ISO/IEC 42001 not yet held. Procurement gate for partners — but note neither cert says whether a patch discharges a GDPR Article 17 obligation. The real gate is the counsel opinion + independent third-party validation.
  • The demo gap is an existence risk: the flagship example (Paris→capital on gemma-4-E2B-it) is the easiest possible case — public fact, single high-salience triple, known target, no adversary. Personal data is diffuse, redundantly encoded, and not representable as one clean triple. The 30-day experiment (§9) answers whether the technique transfers to erasing a person at all — it is a go/no-go gate, not a marketing task.

7. Pricing — anchor to retrain/N, not a budget line

The substitute is retraining: EDPB and CNIL treat retrain-and-attest as the currently defensible path, so the alternative to an erasure is re-running the fine-tune without the data [REG-06]. The corrected measured anchor is a function of dataset scale, not one number: $250–700 covers a small-SFT 70B pass only (8xH100; $45–180 QLoRA); production scale (100M tokens) is $2k–3k, serious scale (1B tokens) is $7k–51.2k, and institutional scale (500M–1B tokens, including engineering) is $15k–45k [MKT-01]. For non-trivial enterprise work, that is $0.5k–50k compute or $10k–100k as a full engagement [MKT-01]. The legacy $50k–500k band survives only as the all-in full engagement — labor + data prep + engineering time + downtime — not the compute [MKT-02].

Retraining clears the whole backlog once, not per request; therefore per-event pricing must sit far below retrain/N. Assumption: N=4 requests/year (one per quarter) is the smallest realistic planning cadence; where N > 1, pricing one event as a full retrain is off ~10x [MKT-01, MKT-03]. 8. Standards — publish, don't committee

  • The insight stands: no shared evaluation protocol certifies weights-level removal; every vendor's unlearning claim is self-measured; whoever defines the protocol owns the category's credibility. But standards are non-proprietary by construction, and formal bodies (CEN/CENELEC) run on multi-year clocks — EN 18286 took years and its OJ citation is still pending.
  • The play: publish an open evaluation benchmark and protocol — probe suite, attack set, pass/fail thresholds, reference implementation, own results including failures — on arXiv and GitHub within 60 days. ~$50–100k of researcher time. Same work as the §9 experiment, published instead of hidden.
  • Why it is the only genuinely overnight asset: publication is same-day; it unblocks counsel and auditors faster than SOC 2; it makes us the de-facto author when regulators formalize; and it forces Hirundo to adopt our protocol or explain why not. Do it alone, in public — not co-authored with a better-funded competitor who would get equal claim to the category.
