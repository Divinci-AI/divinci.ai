Partner brief for In-Q-Tel: assurance receipts for model erasure

Status: proposal only. Divinci has no existing relationship with IQT, has not applied or been contacted by IQT, and has no government customers as of 2026-09-02 [BOUND-01]. If IQT were interested, this brief outlines a technology development agreement (TDA)—IQT's development and licensing form with a published envelope of $500k–3M [IQT-03]—not an existing programme.

Why IQT is the strategic fit

IQT was founded by the CIA in 1999 as an independent, not-for-profit strategic investment firm and exists to identify, evaluate, and leverage commercial technology for the U.S. national-security community [IQT-01]. Its partner network spans the CIA, NSA, NGA, DIA, FBI, DHS, Cyber Command, Space Force and allied intelligence communities [IQT-02]. Its 2026 outlook names AI among the capability areas it watches [IQT-07]. The fit offered here is narrow: not another model, but an independently checkable assurance artifact for the problem of removing a remembered item from a model and proving what remains.

The sourced government problem—and the boundary around our remedy

A 17 March 2026 Technology Review report says the Pentagon is discussing secure environments where generative-AI companies would train military-specific models on classified data; the stated risk is that "sensitive intelligence like surveillance reports or battlefield assessments could become embedded into the models themselves" [NEED-01]. The article does not state a need for unlearning or erasure. Erasure-with-receipt as a remedy is our inference, not a stated government requirement. Classified-data spillage is also a known federal control problem, but the listed controls are preventive and pre-training; that source is context only [NEED-02].

The capability Divinci has shipped

  • Signed receipts are in production. They use Ed25519; a published verifier is MIT-licensed and has no Divinci dependency [PROD-01]. This is the artifact an auditor keeps after erasure.
  • QA re-measurement gates are shipped, including named metrics and minimum-score / maximum-drift thresholds [PROD-03].
  • The erasure engine is staging only, does not affect live chat traffic, and exactly one vIndex is served [PROD-04]. We would describe it as a pilot, not production erasure.
  • We report a negative result. In August, corpus erasure on published position papers reduced target retrieval from 4/4 to 0 while full-corpus QA fell 0.926 to 0.876; controls degraded more than the erased topic (-0.058 versus -0.026) [PROD-09]. That test was not model weights and not personal data.
  • We avoid unsupported platform claims. We do not claim hash-chained receipts, externally anchored certificate chains, bit-for-bit restore, or reversibility in both directions [PROD-06].

These capabilities matter because IQT Labs audits AI tools under the AI Ethics Framework for the Intelligence Community [IQT-05], including RoBERTa, FakeFinder, and SkyScan with reported attack classes including data poisoning and model evasion [IQT-05]. Its published auditing lessons emphasize going beyond accuracy, auditing the use case, and looking for vulnerabilities across the ML stack [IQT-06]. A signed receipt and independently licensed verifier make the use case auditable: the report records what was requested, what was erased, the re-measure result, and what the model can still do.

Work program if IQT funds it

What IQT would fund: Phase 1–3 only—an unclassified assurance pilot that does not use classified data. What IQT would not fund: production erasure, classified-data work, or any prior commitment by a government partner. The purpose is to test whether the shipped receipt/QA pipeline can produce IQT-style assurance evidence for the NEED-01 exposure before any sponsor or classified-environment conversation.

Proposed TDA scope (not agreed): we would propose a $750k–1.2M TDA, at the low end of IQT's published $500k–3M range [IQT-03]. Assumption: this is a planning-grade funding proposal, not an agreed amount; exact scope depends on IQT and partner review. Planned timeline for the funded programme: Phase 1 months 1–2, Phase 2 months 3–7, Phase 3 months 8–10 with final report acceptance closing month 10; months 11–12 are reserved only for the conditional Phase 4 follow-on below, if separately approved. The month allocation is a proposal, not a commitment.

  • Phase 1 — pre-registered evaluation plan, months 1–2. Deliverable: a public evaluation plan consistent with the plan's pre-registered GDPR Article 17 gate [GATE-01]. Named metrics, planning-grade thresholds, a kill criterion, multiple models and association types, and at least one control individual are fixed before testing. Success is the gate's own pre-committed metrics; a result that fails the kill criterion stops the line.
  • Phase 2 — erasure assurance run, months 3–7. Deliverable: an open-weight erasure experiment on public or synthetic data, with baseline, erase, re-measure, and signed receipt. A named control remains untouched. The government partner receives the protocol, the result—including any failure—and the signed receipt, while IQT receives independently checkable assurance artifacts. Acceptance criteria are the gate's pre-registered planning thresholds [GATE-01] — Assumption: target and paraphrase-restatement recall <0.05 of baseline, control-individual recall ≥0.95, held-out QA ≥0.95, and maxDrift ≤0.05 are planning-grade decision thresholds, not measured product results — measured with the QA re-measurement gates that are already shipped [PROD-03]; any failure is reported, not hidden.
  • Phase 3 — assurance audit report, months 8–10. Deliverable: a report mapped to IQT Labs' assurance framework and auditing lessons [IQT-05, IQT-06]. It would explicitly separate the negative August result, measured findings, assumptions, and interpretations. A 2023 lesson is particularly relevant: a model can be accurate yet biased, so success is not a single accuracy score [IQT-06]. Its Phase 3 acceptance criterion is the independently run verifier passing with zero Divinci dependency [PROD-01].
  • Phase 4 — conditional follow-on, months 11–12 only if separately approved. Only after Phase 1–3 and verified eligibility, a cleared sponsor or sponsor-provided environment could explore the actual NEED-01 exposure. We make no classified work commitment here.

Assumption: the $750k–1.2M figure above is our planning-grade proposal within the published TDA envelope, not a measured programme cost; it would be re-scoped with IQT and the partner-problem owner before any agreement [IQT-03]. Equity-only terms are a published IQT structure of $250,000–$500,000 [IQT-03], but we are proposing a TDA because the requested output is development, licensing, and assurance artifacts—not a capital investment.

How success is measured

We would pre-register the same falsifiable style as the plan's gate [GATE-01]: erasure effectiveness, utility retention, control stability, direct/reworded prompt transfer, and an independently verifier-checkable receipt chain of custody. If the test fails, the signed artifacts still have value: they show the failure and prevent an unsupported claim.

Why not a generic investment pitch

The precedent from IQT Labs is audits, not endorsements [IQT-05]. The proposal therefore asks IQT to fund a bounded, falsifiable assurance pilot with named deliverables and a signed, independently verifiable artifact. If IQT's use-case audit shows that the capability does not transfer, that would be a useful negative result—and we would publish it.
