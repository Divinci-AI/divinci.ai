1. Company Overview

Name and founding date?

Divinci AI Inc., a Delaware C-corporation (file no. 2970809), incorporated 22 January 2024. Platform development began mid-2023. Headquartered in Irvine, California; web: divinci.ai.

Mission statement or vision?

Custom AIs a business can prove: built, continuously evolved, and independently verifiable on the Divinci platform.

High-level description of the product or service offered?

The Divinci platform builds and runs custom AI assistants for businesses: retrieval over their own content, voice, web embeds, and SDK, CLI and MCP surfaces. The compliance wedge, Model Erasure with a receipt, finds what an AI model has learned about a person, removes it, proves the rest of the model still performs, and hands the auditor a signed receipt they can verify without trusting Divinci.

2. Problem

What market pain points or customer challenges does the company solve?

A company can delete a database row, but a model that learned from that data can still remember the person in its learned numerical memory. Under GDPR Article 17 (the right to erasure) "we deleted the row" is no longer a complete answer, and regulators (EDPB, CNIL) currently treat retraining as the defensible response: slow, expensive, and in practice rarely done. Most large enterprises running AI in Europe have no answer if a model-level erasure demand arrives. No regulator has yet ruled on whether any unlearning method discharges Article 17; that question is open for the whole market.

Data or statistics on the problem's scale or urgency?

EU AI Act enforcement powers went live 2 August 2026. GDPR fines run up to €20M or 4% of global turnover. Retraining a 70B model costs $0.5k–50k in compute for a non-trivial fine-tune and $50k–500k as a full engagement including labor, data preparation, engineering time and downtime.

3. Solution and benefits

How does the product address the problem?

Find, remove, prove. Identify what a model learned about an individual; remove it on open-weight models; re-measure the model against pre-set performance thresholds; issue a signed receipt (Ed25519, a standard digital-signature method) served from a public endpoint and checked by an open-source verifier with no dependency on Divinci.

Unique selling propositions or innovations versus competitors?

The proof step is the product. In Divinci's own August 2026 test the untouched control topics degraded more than the erased topic (−0.058 vs −0.026), which a vendor that cannot measure would have shipped silently. Nobody else sells the measurement half. The receipt is live in production today, not a proposed format.

Benefits?

Discharge an erasure demand without a full retrain. Evidence an auditor can check alone. Damage to the model is measured rather than assumed. The result is a single artifact a regulator, auditor or customer can verify independently.

10. Competitive Advantage

Key differentiators from competitors?

The bundle: baseline, erase, re-measure, attest, delivered as one verifiable artifact. Hirundo (Israel, $8M seed, weight-level edits on Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen and Gemma) is ahead on distribution and could copy a receipt format in weeks, but is behind on EU framing and, as far as Divinci can see, on the evidence artifact. Divinci publishes its failures, which is credibility rather than a moat.

Proprietary technology, patents or barriers to entry?

Rank-one editing methods (ROME, MEMIT) are published commodity; the moat is the measured, signed, independently checkable evidence around the edit, and the production receipt infrastructure already running. Registered trademark; no patents filed; a freedom-to-operate review is budgeted.
