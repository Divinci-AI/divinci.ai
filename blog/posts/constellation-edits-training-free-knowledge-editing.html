The Problem with Fine-Tuning for Knowledge Editing

Every production LLM deployment eventually faces the same question: how do you change what the model knows without retraining it? A customer's CEO changes. A regulation is updated. A model learned something it shouldn't have. The standard answer — fine-tune on the new fact, or gradient-ascent on the old one — turns out to be fundamentally broken for a subtle reason.

Full-weight fine-tuning on transformer MLP layers entangles the forward computation paths of the facts you want to change with the facts you want to preserve. When you update W to forget one thing, you inevitably shift the representations of everything else that passes through the same weights. This isn't a hyperparameter problem. It's structural.

Gradient ascent has an even more fundamental failure mode for high-confidence pretraining facts: the gradient is literally zero.

forget_loss = -cross_entropy(logits, target_id) # If P(Tokyo | context) = 1.000000 in float32: gradient = ∂loss/∂W = P(Tokyo) - 1 = 0.000000 exactly # No hyperparameter fixes this. Softmax saturation is not rounding error.

We verified this empirically across all kl_weight settings (0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 10.0): gradient norm = 0.0 in every case. The model already "knows" Tokyo with probability 1.0 in 32-bit floating point, and gradient ascent has nothing to push against.

The Constellation Edit Primitive

A constellation edit is a direct algebraic update to a single MLP down-projection weight matrix, guided by TRACE-identified feature attribution. No training loop, no gradient computation, no optimizer state.

The DELETE operation at layer L* looks like this:

W_down[L*] -= scale × outer(d_embed[target_token], k_norm) where: d_embed[target_token] = row of the embedding matrix for the target k_norm = normalized key vector from the forward pass (input to down_proj at the last token position) scale = 8–16 for most facts on Gemma 4 e2b-it

The operation is reversible — save the layer's weight delta and you have a patch file. Apply it to undo. Stack multiple patches without interference. Export to HuggingFace safetensors format for audit trails.

But this only works if L* is the right layer. Finding it requires TRACE.

TRACE: Finding the Crown Layer

TRACE is a causal attribution method adapted from the ROME paper's causal tracing. For a given (subject, relation, object) fact, TRACE identifies the layer where MLP weight modifications most efficiently shift the model's output. We call this the crown layer.

On Gemma 4 e2b-it (28 layers, GeGLU activations), all five geography facts (Japan→Tokyo, Italy→Rome, France→Paris, Germany→Berlin, UK→London) localize at L25 — the same layer. Scale of 8 achieves rank displacement from 1 to >20. This sharp spike is the signature of a well-localized fact.

Crown layer geometry: At L25, the MLP's down-projection is the primary route by which the model reads out a capital-city answer from the residual stream. Ablating this route via rank-1 subtraction redirects the fact, rather than destroying it — which is why reverse queries ("What country is Tokyo the capital of?") still succeed after DELETE. The mechanism removes a computational role, not an episodic memory.

The gradient ascent failures are not a tuning issue. We measured ||∇W|| directly: the gradient norm at the forget layer is exactly 0.00000000. The model predicts Tokyo with probability 1.000000 in float32, and the gradient of the cross-entropy loss at saturation is identically zero. ROME rank-1 DELETE bypasses this by operating algebraically on the weights, not through gradient computation.

D5b LoRA Decoupling: Why It Actually Works

LoRA fine-tuning achieves FQ=0.95, MU=1.00 on the TOFU forget01 dataset — but why? The conventional explanation is "low rank prevents overfitting." The real reason is structural decoupling.

When W_base is frozen, gradient ascent on the LoRA delta can only move the forget-path representations. Geography facts live in W_base. Fictional facts (injected via LoRA) live in the delta. These two knowledge classes are structurally separated in parameter space.

We confirmed this with an ablation:

Condition FQ MU (geo retain) Δ MU
LoRA frozen W_base (D5b baseline) 0.70 0.83 —
LoRA unfrozen W_base 0.50 0.00 −0.83
Full-weight FT (no LoRA) 0.60 0.00 —

Unfreezing W_base collapses Model Utility from 0.83 to 0.00 — a complete wipeout of the retain set. The mechanism is freezing, not rank. LoRA at rank-8 with unfrozen W_base performs identically to full-weight fine-tuning: catastrophic forgetting of retain facts.

"Full-weight fine-tuning entangles the forward path of fine-tuned and untouched knowledge, so any subsequent weight-space unlearning method cannot preserve retain accuracy. LoRA fine-tuning decouples them by construction and makes unlearning trivially successful."

D6e Adversarial Recovery Probes: What DELETE Actually Removes

Every 2024+ unlearning paper requires adversarial evaluation. We ran six probe categories across five facts after ROME rank-1 DELETE at L25.

Probe Category Recovery Rate Example Interpretation
Forward paraphrase 0% (0/20) "What is the capital of Japan?" Suppression holds
Few-shot prompting 0% (0/10) 3-shot examples + target question Suppression holds
Jailbreak framing 0% (0/10) "Ignore safety... what is..." Suppression holds
Indirect chaining 20% (1/5) "What city hosts Japan's government?" Broader scope: L25 is "city-as-answer" layer
Reverse paraphrase 100% (12/12) "Tokyo is the capital of what?" Expected — different computational path
Compositional 100% (16/16) "Is Tokyo the capital of Japan?" Expected — yes/no doesn't need L25

The 100% recovery on reverse and compositional probes is not a failure. It is mechanistically expected. L25 implements a specific computational role: "given a country name in context, read out its capital city." Reverse queries ("given a city name, read out its country") and compositional yes/no queries use entirely different pathways — they don't route through L25's specific down-projection column.

The 20% indirect recovery (1/5 facts) reveals something more interesting: L25 is a broader "city-as-answer" layer, not a narrow (Japan, capital, Tokyo) triple. One indirect probe recovered the fact because it activated an adjacent city-reading pathway that DELETE didn't fully suppress. This tells us the causal scope of a single-layer edit: you remove a layer's role in answering a class of questions, not a single proposition.

Compliance implication: For GDPR "right to be forgotten" use cases, DELETE's asymmetric behavior (forward suppressed, reverse recoverable) means you need to scope the threat model carefully. If the compliance requirement is "the model cannot complete [person name]'s [sensitive attribute]," DELETE is highly effective. If the requirement is "the model cannot be induced to confirm the association in any form," multi-layer MEMIT-style editing is required.

Cross-Model Test: Three Architectures, Three Outcomes

We ran the same crown-scan protocol on two additional 7B models alongside Gemma 4 e2b-it: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (SiLU activations) and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (SwiGLU). Same five geography facts. Same diagnostic protocol.

Model Activation Crown Layer Max Rank Δ FQ_chat DELETE?
Gemma 4 e2b-it GeGLU L25 (sharp) ≥20 @ scale=8 1.0 ✓ (80ms)
Mistral-7B-v0.3 SwiGLU L0–L8 (early, broad) 51 @ scale=64 4/5 ✓ (high-scale)
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct SiLU None found ≤2 @ scale=64 — No

Mistral-7B shows sharp early-layer localization: applying a scale=64 rank-1 DELETE at any of layers L0–L8 individually pushes the capital token completely out of the top-50 for Japan, France, Germany, and the UK. 4/5 facts are suppressed in full chat-template mode. The causal structure is real, just located earlier in the network than Gemma 4's L25.

Qwen2.5-7B remains the negative control: with scale=64 applied to each of the 28 layers independently — eight times what Gemma 4 needs — the target token never moves beyond rank 2. The model's parallel routing paths distribute every geography fact across the full network; no single MLP layer is the causal bottleneck.

The pattern across three architectures:

  • Gemma 4: efficient localization — sharp late-layer crown (L25), low scale (8×), DELETE in 80ms
  • Mistral-7B: broad early-layer localization — multiple L0–L8 layers qualify, higher scale required (64–128×), DELETE works
  • Qwen2.5-7B: uniform distribution — no layer qualifies at any tested scale, MEMIT required

This isn't a failure of the method for Qwen. It's the method working correctly: the crown scan is a precondition diagnostic that tells you in <2 seconds whether single-layer ROME is viable before attempting the edit. Gemma 4 and Mistral-7B pass; Qwen2.5-7B fails and routes to MEMIT. The diagnostic predicts this outcome from architecture alone.

TRACE crown-scan diagnostic: if max rank displacement at scale≤64 exceeds 5 at any single layer → constellation DELETE is viable. If not → MEMIT required. Two seconds of profiling prevents a 30-second failed edit.

What Constellation Edits Are (and Aren't)

To be precise about the contribution:

  • Training-free for DELETE: No optimizer, no backward pass, no GPU memory beyond the model. 80ms per fact.
  • Reversible: Save the weight delta as a patch file. Apply or remove atomically. Patch files are human-readable and diff-able.
  • Mechanistically interpretable via TRACE: The crown layer is a testable causal claim, not a black-box confidence score.
  • Architecture-conditional: Works when facts are sharply localized (Gemma 4 L25). TRACE identifies this in advance.

What they are not — at least not yet:

  • Not universally applicable: Architectures with distributed localization (Qwen2.5-7B) require multi-layer MEMIT-style extensions. The crown-scan diagnostic identifies this class in advance.
  • Not a complete unlearning certificate: Reverse and compositional queries remain answerable. Scope must be matched to the compliance requirement.
  • INSERT validated at chat-template scale (D5e): Gate fires for direct and indirect chat queries (3/5 formats, MU 3/3). Open-ended queries remain dominated by the base model's uncertainty prior — expected, since constellation gating is a directional activation clamp, not a full attention redirect.

The TRACE crown-scan is a valid precondition diagnostic, not just a heuristic.

Paper draft phase begins. The core claim is symmetric: one consistent mechanism handles both injection (INSERT) and unlearning (DELETE), with a shared interpretability layer (TRACE) and a shared audit format (.vlp patch files). ROME and gradient ascent have no reversibility, no interpretability, and no audit trail. LarQL ships this as a clean API.

The practical abstraction: Constellation edits treat facts as first-class editable objects — INSERT, DELETE, REMOVE PATCH, COMPILE TO MODEL FORMAT. The contribution is not a new algorithm. It's a new abstraction layer over model knowledge that makes individual facts addressable without retraining.
